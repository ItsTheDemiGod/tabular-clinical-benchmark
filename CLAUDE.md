# tabular-clinical-benchmark

## 1. Project in one paragraph
Conference-paper project by Demian (B.Tech AI and ML student, Karunya Institute, Coimbatore). Working title: "When Does Deep Learning Help on Small Clinical Tabular Data? A Multi-Dataset Study of Accuracy, Calibration, Robustness and Explanation Agreement". Goal: a benchmark of classical ML, tabular deep learning and (optionally) a tabular foundation model on small public clinical datasets, with a rigorous, leakage-safe protocol, aimed at a conference submission around the start of December 2026 (target conference not yet chosen).

Research questions:
- RQ1 (data size): how do model families compare as the training set shrinks from 100% to 10%?
- RQ2 (reliability): beyond AUROC, how do models differ in calibration (Brier, log loss, ECE) and training time?
- RQ3 (robustness): which models degrade most under missing values and noisy inputs?
- RQ4 (explanations): do the best models of each family agree on which features matter (SHAP), and are those clinically plausible?

## 4. Datasets and data rules (`src/data.py`)
Five public binary datasets, loaded by `load_dataset(name, verbose=True)` returning `(X, y, meta)`. Sizes below are after cleaning and deduplication, and were re-verified as of 2026-11-07 against `results/dataset_summary.csv` and `load_dataset(...)` directly:
- `pima_diabetes` (OpenML "diabetes" v1, id 37): 768 rows, 8 numeric, 34.9% positive. Zeros in `plas`, `pres`, `skin`, `insu`, `mass` are really missing and are replaced by NaN (counts confirmed via `meta["zeros_replaced_with_nan"]`: plas 5, pres 35, skin 227, insu 374, mass 11; 10.6% missing overall). `preg`, `pedi` and `age` are untouched.
- `heart_cleveland` (UCI id 45, 303 x 13): target binarised as `(num > 0)` (any disease = 1), confirmed in `src/data.py`. Categorical: `sex`, `cp`, `fbs`, `restecg`, `exang`, `slope`, `thal` (7 columns). Numeric: `age`, `trestbps`, `chol`, `thalach`, `oldpeak`, `ca` (6 columns). Missing (confirmed): `thal` 2, `ca` 4; 0.15% missing overall.
- `ckd` (UCI id 336): 400 rows, 14 numeric and 10 categorical, 62.5% positive, 10.5% missing. `sg`, `al`, `su` are ordered scales and are kept NUMERIC on purpose (`meta["ordinal_as_numeric"]`, confirmed in `src/data.py`). This dataset is nearly saturated (mean AUROC 0.9999 for logreg/rf, confirmed from `results/all_runs.csv`), so it separates models poorly on AUROC; rely on log loss, Brier and ECE for it, and report statistics with and without CKD.
- `ilpd` (UCI id 225): 583 raw rows, 13 exact duplicates dropped, 570 kept (confirmed in `results/dataset_summary.csv`: `n_rows_before_dedup=583`, `n_duplicates_found=13`, `n_rows=570`). The dropped rows may be different patients with identical values (no patient ID); dropping is the safer choice against train and test leakage.
- `breast_wisconsin` (UCI id 17): 569 rows, 30 numeric, malignant = 1.

Rules: raw downloads cached in `data/` must never be modified (verified by SHA-256 earlier, not re-checked as of 2026-11-07). All categorical columns are plain object dtype with `np.nan` for missing (never pandas string dtype, `pd.NA` or the literal strings `"nan"`, `"None"`, `"<NA>"`, `"?"` — enforced by `_normalize_categoricals`, which raises on a placeholder string). Exact duplicates are dropped; rows with identical features and conflicting labels are counted but kept (currently zero).

## 5. Protocol conventions (do not change without Demian's decision)
- Splits: `make_splits(X, y, n_splits=5, n_repeats=2, seed=42)` = 10 outer splits per dataset (confirmed signature in `src/utils.py`). `task_seed = 42 + split_idx` (confirmed in `src/runner.py`: `task_seed = seed + split_idx`, default `seed=42`); this value is stored in the "seed" column and seeds the models, tuning and subsampling.
- Preprocessing: ONE shared pipeline for all models (median imputation + optional `StandardScaler` for numeric, most-frequent imputation + one-hot with `handle_unknown="ignore"` for categorical), always fitted on the training fold only (confirmed in `src/preprocessing.py`). No model uses native missing-value handling (XGBoost and LightGBM get imputed data too). A model's input width must ALWAYS come from the fitted preprocessor output (it changes with the training subset, for example Cleveland gives 25 columns at 100% and about 23 at 10%), never from a hardcoded number.
- Training fractions: 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0 of each training fold, stratified, with at least 2 samples per class (`subsample_train`, confirmed).
- Tuning: Optuna TPE, 20 trials, 3 inner folds, mean inner-CV AUROC, same budget for every tunable model, done ONCE at fraction 1.0 on the training fold only, and the tuned hyperparameters are reused for the smaller fractions.
- `effective_params` (`src/models.py`, confirmed): to keep relative regularisation constant at small fractions, `xgb min_child_weight = max(1.0, value * fraction)` (float), `lgbm min_child_samples` and `rf min_samples_leaf = max(1, round(value * fraction))`; `logreg` and all deep models unchanged. At fraction 1.0 params are identical. Reason: leaf-size limits tuned at 100% otherwise forbid any split on tiny subsets (this produced constant predictions in Phase 2a). Limitation: at fraction 0.1 these leaf sizes always floor at 1.
- Deep-model training schedule (`src/deep_models.py`, confirmed): validation split of 15% (`VALIDATION_FRACTION`) taken from the training subset only (never the test fold), via `split_validation` which requires at least 4 samples per class or returns `None`; early stopping on validation log loss with best-weight restore; `effective_batch = min(batch_size, max(8, n_train_part // 4))`; schedule is expressed as `max_steps=600` / `patience_steps=60` module constants, converted internally to epochs (`max_epochs = min(500, ceil(600/steps_per_epoch))`, `patience_epochs = max(5, ceil(60/steps_per_epoch))`); fixed 200 steps (`FIXED_STEPS_NO_VALIDATION`) with no early stopping when a class has fewer than 4 samples. AdamW, gradient clipping 1.0, `BCEWithLogitsLoss`. The tree models have no early stopping (asymmetry, state it in the paper). TabNet: `_avoid_singleton_remainder` nudges `effective_batch` and `virtual_batch_size` up by 1 when the last batch would have exactly 1 row (BatchNorm crash found in Phase 2b). FT-Transformer is our own implementation and treats ALL encoded columns as numeric tokens (simplification).
- Metrics (`src/metrics.py`, confirmed): `auroc`, `f1` and `balanced_accuracy` at threshold 0.5, `brier`, `log_loss` (probabilities clipped to `[1e-7, 1-1e-7]`), `ece` = binary ECE on the positive-class probability with 10 equal-width bins on `[0,1]`, computed manually (not sklearn's calibration module). Single-class `y_true` raises an error.
- Result row schema (`results/raw/*.csv`, never change the order — confirmed against `RAW_COLUMNS` in `src/runner.py`): `dataset, model, split_idx, fraction, seed, n_train, n_test, n_features_encoded, auroc, f1, balanced_accuracy, brier, log_loss, ece, fit_time_s, predict_time_s, n_unique_probs, constant_prediction, status, error, params_json`. `params_json` is a JSON object with keys `"tuned"`, `"effective"` and `"fraction"`. A constant-prediction row keeps status "ok" but is flagged, and calibration analyses must exclude or separately flag such rows (confirmed: zero such rows in the current `results/all_runs.csv`, 1400/1400 rows `status="ok"`, 0 flagged `constant_prediction`). Failed fractions are written as status "failed" with the error text, never skipped.
- Results layout (confirmed file counts as of 2026-11-07): `results/raw` (350 CSVs), `results/tuning` (350 JSONs), `results/predictions` (350 npz), `results/models` (350 joblib, fraction 1.0 only, for robustness and SHAP later), `results/diagnostics` (150 JSONs — deep models only: 5 datasets x 3 deep models x 10 splits), `results/phase2a_all_runs.csv` (800 classical rows, frozen), `results/all_runs.csv` (1400 rows = 350 tasks x 4 fractions), `results/MANIFEST_sha256.txt` (1552 file hashes: the 1550 files in `raw`+`tuning`+`predictions`+`models`+`diagnostics`, plus `phase2a_all_runs.csv` and `all_runs.csv`), `results/phase2c/` (Phase 2c diagnostic outputs, see section 6), `results_smoke` and `results_smoke_2b` (smoke outputs, disposable).
- Fit times were measured with 3 to 4 parallel workers and are only rough.

## 7. Known limitations and open questions (put in the paper's limitations)
- Small public datasets, binary tasks only, no external validation.
- CKD is saturated; ILPD dedup may remove distinct patients; Pima insulin (48.7% missing) and skin (29.6%) are median-imputed without missing-indicators.
- TabNet may be hurt by our schedule. Corrected as of 2026-11-07 from diagnostics JSONs (`results/diagnostics/*__{model}__*.json`, key `"0.1"`): at fraction 0.1, `patience_epochs` is **12 to 20** (not 5 to 8) and the validation part (`n_val`) is **4 to 9 rows** (not 5 to 8) — this applies to all three deep models (mlp/ftt/tabnet), since they share the same `training_schedule` function, not just TabNet. The 600-step cap is correct (`MAX_STEPS=600` in `src/deep_models.py`). About a third of MLP runs at fraction 1.0 hit the step/epoch cap — confirmed: 17/50 (34%) of `mlp` fraction-1.0 tasks have `epochs_run >= max_epochs` in their diagnostics file.
  **Phase 2c sensitivity-check outcome** (`scripts/phase2c_diagnostics.py sensitivity`, 360 fits, splits 0-2 only): TabNet's known collapse on `ckd` at fraction 1.0 (baseline mean AUROC 0.919, split std 0.138) **disappears** under all three longer-schedule/bigger-validation variants tested (`A_long` 2000/150/0.15, `B_bigval` 600/60/0.30, `C_long_bigval` 2000/150/0.30 for max_steps/patience_steps/validation_fraction): mean AUROC 0.999-1.000, std ≤ 0.0015. Dataset-averaged, TabNet gains +0.022 AUROC at fraction 0.1 and +0.016 at fraction 1.0 under `A_long`; MLP and FT-Transformer barely move (every change < 0.01 AUROC) — the schedule mainly affects TabNet. 0/360 fits failed, 0 NaN losses, 0 constant predictions. TabNet's `heart_cleveland` collapses (splits 1 and 7) were only partly covered by this check (split 1 is in scope, split 7 is not, since the brief scoped splits to 0-2) and did not resolve as cleanly there. Full tables: `results/phase2c/sensitivity_report_S1.csv` .. `S5.csv`.
- The classical search-space endpoint table was requested but never reported. Computed as of 2026-11-07 from `results/tuning/*.json` via `scripts/phase2c_diagnostics.py endpoints`: `xgb`'s `min_child_weight` (tuned range 1-10) hits an endpoint in 52% of its 50 tasks (23 at the lower bound of 1, 3 at the upper bound of 10); `rf`'s `min_samples_leaf` (tuned range 1-10) hits an endpoint in 42% of its 50 tasks (18 lower, 3 upper). Both are dominated by the *lower* endpoint, not evenly split. For xgb this is a **mild constraint, not a hard floor**: `min_child_weight` is a hessian-sum threshold, and for logistic loss each row contributes at most `p(1-p) ≤ 0.25` to a leaf's hessian, so `min_child_weight=1` already only requires about 4 or more rows per leaf — the lower bound of 1 is not pinning the tree to trivially small leaves. Full per-model/per-parameter table (34 parameters, 7 models): `results/phase2c/endpoints_overall.csv` and `endpoints_by_dataset.csv`. Parameters at the *same* endpoint in ≥ 15 of 50 tasks: `mlp n_layers` (39: 27 lower/12 upper), `tabnet n_d` (32: 18/14), `ftt n_blocks` (30: 15/15), `ftt d_token` (29: 9/20), `tabnet n_steps` (28: 22/6), `ftt batch_size` (28: 17/11), `xgb min_child_weight` (26: 23/3), `tabnet batch_size` (24: 12/12), `rf min_samples_leaf` (21: 18/3), `mlp batch_size` (20: 13/7), `xgb max_depth` (15: 8/7).
- ECE with 10 bins on **60 to 154** test rows (corrected from "61 to 154" — confirmed `n_test` ranges 60-154 in `results/all_runs.csv`) is noisy; use Brier and log loss as primary calibration measures, with bootstrap confidence intervals.
- Statistics with only 5 datasets have low power; also test across dataset x split results and say so.
