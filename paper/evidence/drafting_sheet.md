# Drafting sheet (Phase 4d): facts only, no paper prose

How to read it. Every row is one fact for one section. `keys` lists numbers-book keys (use them as `\N{key}` in the paper); `values` are their formatted values
(read from `paper/evidence/numbers.json`, never typed); `raw_values` are the unrounded values. Keys starting with `pf:`, `meta:` or `claude:` are verbatim non-numeric
facts from `protocol_facts.json`, the dataset metadata, or CLAUDE.md. `caveat_text` is an exact copy from CLAUDE.md or `claim_map.md`.

Status words (mechanical rule of the claim map, fixed before any value was computed):
- SUPPORTED: the confidence interval excludes 0 in both ALL5 and NO_CKD and the per-dataset signs agree (>=3/5 and >=3/4).
- PARTLY: only part of that rule holds.
- NOT SUPPORTED: the rule fails.
- DESCRIPTIVE: a quantity that is reported, not tested.

Rules: a claim with status NOT SUPPORTED or PARTLY may only be stated with its caveat attached. DESCRIPTIVE items may be stated as numbers only.
Digits in a cell must come from a key's formatted value or a verbatim copy; the `fact_label` column carries only fixed design labels (see tests/test_phase4d.py).

**LIM-003** Limitations item L3 (CLAUDE.md section 7, verbatim)

```text
- TabNet may be hurt by our schedule. Corrected as of 2026-11-07 from diagnostics JSONs (`results/diagnostics/*__{model}__*.json`, key `"0.1"`): at fraction 0.1, `patience_epochs` is **12 to 20** (not 5 to 8) and the validation part (`n_val`) is **4 to 9 rows** (not 5 to 8) — this applies to all three deep models (mlp/ftt/tabnet), since they share the same `training_schedule` function, not just TabNet. The 600-step cap is correct (`MAX_STEPS=600` in `src/deep_models.py`). About a third of MLP runs at fraction 1.0 hit the step/epoch cap — confirmed: 17/50 (34%) of `mlp` fraction-1.0 tasks have `epochs_run >= max_epochs` in their diagnostics file.
  **Phase 2c sensitivity-check outcome** (`scripts/phase2c_diagnostics.py sensitivity`, 360 fits, splits 0-2 only): TabNet's known collapse on `ckd` at fraction 1.0 (baseline mean AUROC 0.919, split std 0.138) **disappears** under all three longer-schedule/bigger-validation variants tested (`A_long` 2000/150/0.15, `B_bigval` 600/60/0.30, `C_long_bigval` 2000/150/0.30 for max_steps/patience_steps/validation_fraction): mean AUROC 0.999-1.000, std ≤ 0.0015. Dataset-averaged, TabNet gains +0.022 AUROC at fraction 0.1 and +0.016 at fraction 1.0 under `A_long`; MLP and FT-Transformer barely move (every change < 0.01 AUROC) — the schedule mainly affects TabNet. 0/360 fits failed, 0 NaN losses, 0 constant predictions. TabNet's `heart_cleveland` collapses (splits 1 and 7) were only partly covered by this check (split 1 is in scope, split 7 is not, since the brief scoped splits to 0-2) and did not resolve as cleanly there. Full tables: `results/phase2c/sensitivity_report_S1.csv` .. `S5.csv`.
```

**LIM-004** Limitations item L4 (CLAUDE.md section 7, verbatim)

```text
- The classical search-space endpoint table was requested but never reported. Computed as of 2026-11-07 from `results/tuning/*.json` via `scripts/phase2c_diagnostics.py endpoints`: `xgb`'s `min_child_weight` (tuned range 1-10) hits an endpoint in 52% of its 50 tasks (23 at the lower bound of 1, 3 at the upper bound of 10); `rf`'s `min_samples_leaf` (tuned range 1-10) hits an endpoint in 42% of its 50 tasks (18 lower, 3 upper). Both are dominated by the *lower* endpoint, not evenly split. For xgb this is a **mild constraint, not a hard floor**: `min_child_weight` is a hessian-sum threshold, and for logistic loss each row contributes at most `p(1-p) ≤ 0.25` to a leaf's hessian, so `min_child_weight=1` already only requires about 4 or more rows per leaf — the lower bound of 1 is not pinning the tree to trivially small leaves. Full per-model/per-parameter table (34 parameters, 7 models): `results/phase2c/endpoints_overall.csv` and `endpoints_by_dataset.csv`. Parameters at the *same* endpoint in ≥ 15 of 50 tasks: `mlp n_layers` (39: 27 lower/12 upper), `tabnet n_d` (32: 18/14), `ftt n_blocks` (30: 15/15), `ftt d_token` (29: 9/20), `tabnet n_steps` (28: 22/6), `ftt batch_size` (28: 17/11), `xgb min_child_weight` (26: 23/3), `tabnet batch_size` (24: 12/12), `rf min_samples_leaf` (21: 18/3), `mlp batch_size` (20: 13/7), `xgb max_depth` (15: 8/7).
```

**LIM-007** Limitations item L7 (CLAUDE.md section 7, verbatim)

```text
- **Calibration caveats (Phase 3b, 2026-11-07; full statement in `results/phase3b/calibration_notes.md`):** every pooled calibration analysis uses 2 x n_rows predictions per (dataset, model, fraction), since each test row appears in 2 of the 10 splits (one per CV repeat). ECE on small samples is biased and noisy, and equal-width (`ece_ew`) and equal-mass (`ece_em`) ECE use different binning rules (they never differed by more than 0.03 in this run, 0/140 cells). The Platt calibration slope/intercept are undefined when the two classes are exactly separable in logit space; at the pooled point-estimate level this never happened in this run (0/140 cells, even for `ckd`), but several `ckd` bootstrap replicates were separable and dropped (up to 101/2000 for `ckd`/logreg at fraction 1.0). 29/280 non-separable (dataset, model, fraction) cells have a pooled slope below 0.5 or above 2 (mostly `ckd` at fraction 1.0, where `logreg`'s slope is 10.78 and `rf`'s is 3.42, and most models at fraction 0.1). The cross-fitted post-hoc Platt/isotonic check (`scripts/phase3b_calibration.py posthoc`) is descriptive only and not part of the pipeline: isotonic regression's AUROC changed by up to 0.059-0.110 (max over individual splits) relative to the original predictions, and `logreg`'s isotonic log loss rose by about +0.21 to +0.26 dataset-averaged (isotonic can map a prediction to exactly 0 or 1 on a held-out fold, which is then clipped and penalised heavily by log loss if wrong) — Platt's fallback-to-identity rate was 439/3500 fold-fits (12.5%, single-class or separable fit folds); isotonic never fell back (0/3500).
```

**LIM-008** Limitations item L8 (CLAUDE.md section 7, verbatim)

```text
- **Robustness caveats (Phase 3c, 2026-11-08; full statement in `results/phase3c/robustness_notes.md`):** every result describes MODEL + the saved pipeline's IMPUTER together (median/most-frequent imputation fitted on the training fold is part of the pipeline), not the model in isolation; tree models use no native NaN handling. Missingness tested is MCAR only — no informative-missingness mechanism (e.g. values missing because they are abnormal) was simulated. Noise is relative to each column's TRAINING standard deviation and applied to ordinal numeric columns (CKD `sg`/`al`/`su`, Cleveland `ca`) as if continuous. 5 independent perturbation draws per (dataset, split, kind, level); every model is evaluated on the identical perturbed matrix for a given draw. The dataset-averaged bootstrap resamples the 10 splits within each dataset and ignores between-dataset variability. Degradation is always relative to each model's OWN clean performance on that split, so a model with lower clean performance can show smaller absolute degradation.
```

**LIM-009** Limitations item L9 (CLAUDE.md section 7, verbatim)

```text
- **Explanation and noise-variant caveats (Phase 3d, 2026-11-08; full statements in `results/phase3d/noise_continuous/robustness_notes.md` and `results/phase3d/explanation_notes.md`):** `logreg` is explained in LOG-ODDS (`decision_function`) while `rf`/`mlp` are explained in PROBABILITY of the positive class, so only RANKINGS (top-5 sets, Spearman correlation) are ever compared across models, never raw SHAP magnitudes. The `mlp` KernelExplainer uses a 50-row TRAINING background (seed 42 + split_idx) and `nsamples="auto"`. The 10 splits of a dataset come from repeated cross-validation with overlapping training sets and are therefore not independent (same caveat as Phase 3a's BLOCK unit). No direction-of-effect (positive/negative SHAP) check was performed. Correlated features — especially Breast Wisconsin's mean/standard-error/worst triplets — can substitute for one another in a top-5 set (confirmed: `breast_wisconsin`/logreg had 8 distinct top-5 sets and `breast_wisconsin`/mlp had 9, out of 10 splits). For the continuous-only noise variant: CKD's `bp` column was kept IN (noised) despite meeting the same <=10-distinct-values rule as the excluded `sg`/`al`/`su`, per Demian's explicit confirmation, not an automatic decision.
```

**LIM-010** Limitations item L10 (CLAUDE.md section 7, verbatim)

```text
- **Decisions recorded 2026-11-07 (after Phase 2c/3a):**
  1. The deep models are **not** rerun with a longer schedule; the primary results use the baseline training schedule (`MAX_STEPS=600`, `PATIENCE_STEPS=60`, `VALIDATION_FRACTION=0.15`). The Phase 2c 360-fit schedule-sensitivity check is reported only as a supplementary analysis, with the caveats that it used 3 splits (0-2, not all 10), fractions 0.1 and 1.0 only (not 0.25/0.5), no re-tuning of hyperparameters under the new schedule, and that the schedule variants were not chosen by looking at test AUROC.
  2. The classical `xgb` `min_child_weight` wide-range check (0.1 to 10 log-uniform, 3 datasets x 3 splits; results in `results/phase2c/xgb_wide_range.csv`, **not** `results/phase3c`) showed **no consistent change** in test AUROC — the sign of (wide AUROC − original AUROC) is mixed across datasets, splits and fractions. No classical rerun is planned.
  3. Endpoint findings by the same-endpoint rule (full table: `results/phase2c/endpoints_overall.csv`): `xgb min_child_weight` 23/50 at the lower bound; `mlp n_layers` 27/50 lower; `tabnet n_steps` 22/50 lower; `ftt d_token` 20/50 at the upper bound (64, the top of its `[16, 32, 64]` choices — a limitation of possible truncation applies only to `ftt d_token`, since it is the only one of these five where the upper endpoint, not the lower, dominates); `rf min_samples_leaf` 18/50 lower; `ftt batch_size` 17/50 lower; `ftt n_blocks` 15/50 lower and 15/50 upper (split evenly).
```
